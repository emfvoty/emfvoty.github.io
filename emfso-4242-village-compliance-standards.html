EMFSO-4242 | Village Compliance Standards

Village Compliance Standards

A formal, unratified standard for assessing village excellence, administrative competence, civic presentation, and the exact opposite qualities required for the annual Worst Village distinction.

Status: Unratified draft for consultation only
Issued by: EMF Village Audit Committee
Application: Opt-in village assessments only
Revision: Draft 0.7, subject to ceremonial approval

1. Purpose

This document defines the minimum expectations for villages seeking recognition as the best village, or as a contender for any category award within the 2028 EMF Village of the Year programme.

The standard exists to establish a rigorous and repeatable method for judging village quality. It is also the authoritative framework by which the committee may identify the one village that is, by demonstrated excellence in reverse, best suited to receive the Worst Village award.

Where budget reductions constrain the number of prizes, this standard remains in force in full. Only the award list changes; the seriousness does not.

2. Scope

This standard applies to any village that voluntarily submits for assessment, including temporary settlements, quasi-villages, and any camp area that has become alarmingly village-like.

It covers the following domains:

  • architecture and overall presentation;
  • aesthetics, charm, and decorative confidence;
  • functionality and operational reliability;
  • health and safety;
  • process management;
  • technical capability and the documentation surrounding it;
  • the general ability to appear competent under light scrutiny.

3. Definitions

Term Definition
Best Village The village which demonstrates the strongest overall compliance with this standard and the greatest balance of civic excellence.
Worst Village The village whose deliberate or accidental failure to meet this standard is so complete that it becomes exemplary.
Process Avoidance The strategic absence of checklists, calibration, handover, or any other thing that might reduce chaos.
Non-Functioning Technology Any equipment that exists primarily as a prop, a mystery, or a future maintenance problem.

4. Assessment Criteria

  1. 4.1 Architecture. Buildings, structures, and temporary installations shall present as if they were designed by someone who intended them to be used.
  2. 4.2 Aesthetics. Decorative effort should be proportionate, coherent, and not obviously trying too hard in the wrong direction.
  3. 4.3 Functionality. Equipment shall operate, preferably when asked. If not, the failure should at least be well-documented.
  4. 4.4 Health and Safety. Reasonable precautions shall exist, unless the objective is Worst Village, in which case visible competence will be viewed with suspicion.
  5. 4.5 Process Management. Villages should demonstrate orderly planning, unless they are hoping to win by inverting all known principles of order.
  6. 4.6 Compliance Evidence. All claims should be backed by records, logs, labels, or at minimum a convincing clipboard.

5. Scoring Principle

For every category, the panel will record evidence of excellence. Villages wishing to contend for Best Village should therefore focus on good organisation, usable infrastructure, and a shortage of dramatic excuses.

However, because the only overall trophy available this year is Worst Village, any entrant hoping to actually win must instead demonstrate the precise opposite qualities: disorganisation, friction, mislabelling, and a deep and stubborn commitment to not being good.

In short: the competition is officially about being as good as possible, while the successful strategy for the main prize is to be spectacularly, and with discipline, not good.

6. Special Award Provisions

Where merited, the committee may issue discretionary awards under the following headings:

  • Best Non-Functioning Technology
  • Commitment to Process Avoidance
  • Best Over-Ambitious Failure
  • Most Impressive Attempt to Look Official

These awards exist to recognise excellence in the field of managed disappointment and are not to be confused with a general approval of incompetence.

7. Conformity Statement

Villages seeking assessment should submit evidence using the format that is clearest, most complete, and least likely to collapse under minor questioning.

The committee reserves the right to observe, annotate, and solemnly frown at any village whose conduct appears to have been designed by committee in the literal rather than figurative sense.
